Budget 2027 Explained: What It Means for Your Tax in Ireland

Updated 12 November 2026 8 min read TaxBridge Team
Updated for Budget 2027

Budget 2027 was announced on 6 October 2026, and most of its tax changes take effect on 1 January 2027. The Government says a worker earning €50,000 will pay more than €700 less in income tax and USC next year. Here is what changes for workers, renters, the self-employed and businesses, and what you should do now.

Key Takeaways

  • Personal, PAYE and Earned Income tax credits each rise by €125 to €2,125
  • The 20% standard rate band rises by €2,500 to €46,500 (single)
  • The 2% USC band is widened to €30,300
  • The Rent Tax Credit rises to €1,150 per person (€2,300 per couple) for 2027 and 2028
  • The minimum wage rises to €14.94 per hour from 1 January 2027
  • Capital Gains Tax falls from 33% to 31% for disposals from 7 October 2026

Income Tax and USC Changes

The headline measure is a €1.3 billion income tax package. The rates themselves stay at 20% and 40%, but you can earn more before paying the higher rate, and every main tax credit goes up.

USC rates are unchanged at 0.5%, 2%, 3% and 8%. The 2% band was widened so that workers on the higher minimum wage do not drift into the 3% band.

Renters, Buyers and Homeowners

  • Rent Tax Credit: rises by €150 to €1,150 per renter, or €2,300 for a jointly assessed couple, for 2027 and 2028.
  • Help to Buy: the maximum relief for first-time buyers rises by €5,000 to €35,000 from 7 October 2026. The €500,000 property value cap is unchanged.
  • Rent-a-Room relief: the tax-free limit rises from €14,000 to €16,000 from 1 January 2027.
  • Mortgage Interest Tax Relief: was not extended. The 2026 tax year is the last one you can claim, so make sure you claim it in 2027.

Workers and Employers

  • Minimum wage: rises by 79 cent to €14.94 per hour from 1 January 2027.
  • Employer PRSI: the weekly threshold for the lower employer rate rises from €552 to €600.
  • PRSI rates: under the Government's separate PRSI roadmap, employee PRSI rose from 4.2% to 4.35% on 1 October 2026, with a further increase scheduled for 1 October 2027.

Self-Employed and Businesses

  • Corporation tax stays at 12.5%, and there is no change to VAT rates or registration thresholds.
  • Capital Gains Tax falls from 33% to 31% for disposals on or after 7 October 2026. Development land stays at 33%.
  • Start-up company relief is extended to 31 December 2030.
  • The small company preliminary tax threshold rises from €200,000 to €350,000.
  • The R&D tax credit is enhanced, with higher subcontracting limits and a higher first-year payment threshold.
  • Professional Services Withholding Tax will move from a flat 20% to personalised rates, subject to a commencement order.

Savings, Investments and Inheritance

  • Inheritance and gift tax (CAT) thresholds rise from 7 October 2026: Group A (parents to children) to €420,000, Group B to €44,000 and Group C to €22,000. The 33% rate is unchanged.
  • New Savings and Investment Account from 1 July 2027: you can contribute up to €12,000 a year. Balances up to €50,000 are tax-free, and a flat 1% tax applies above that.
  • The tax rate on Irish and EU investment funds and certain life policies falls from 38% to 35% from 1 January 2027.

Welfare and Families

  • Core weekly social welfare payments and pensions rise by €10.
  • The Child Support Payment rises by €6 a week, the Fuel Allowance by €5, and the Living Alone Allowance by €3.
  • A new €500 cost-of-disability payment for people on long-term disability payments.
  • Maximum childcare fees fall to €550 per month from September 2027.
  • The third-level student contribution falls by €150 to €2,350.

What Should You Do Now?

  1. Claim 2022 before 31 December 2026. After that date, any refund due for 2022 is lost for good.
  2. Check your January payslip. Your new credits and rate band should appear automatically through your Revenue Payroll Notification.
  3. Claim your Rent Tax Credit for 2026 now, and for 2027 during the year through myAccount.
  4. Claim Mortgage Interest Tax Relief for 2026, the last year it is available.
  5. Employers: update pay rates for the €14.94 minimum wage from 1 January.
Please note

These figures are based on the Budget 2027 announcements. Some measures still need to pass through the Finance Bill 2026 and Social Welfare Bill, so details may be adjusted. We will update this guide if anything changes.
