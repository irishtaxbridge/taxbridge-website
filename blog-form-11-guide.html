Self-Employed in Ireland? Your Form 11 Guide for 2027

Updated 12 November 2026 8 min read TaxBridge Team
Updated for Budget 2027

If you are self-employed, a landlord or a proprietary director, you must file a Form 11 each year. Budget 2027 brings a higher Earned Income Tax Credit, a wider rate band and a wider USC band, while Class S PRSI continues to rise. Here is what you need to know for your 2026 return and 2027 planning.

Key Takeaways

  • Your 2026 return and 2027 preliminary tax are due by 31 October 2027
  • ROS users normally get an extension to mid-November
  • The Earned Income Tax Credit rises to €2,125 in 2027
  • Class S PRSI is 4.35% from 1 October 2026, rising again in October 2027
  • Late filing surcharges are 5% or 10% of your tax bill

Who Must File a Form 11?

  • Sole traders and other self-employed people
  • Landlords with rental income
  • Proprietary directors (more than 15% of a company)
  • PAYE workers with net non-PAYE income of €5,000 or more, or gross non-PAYE income over €30,000
  • Anyone with foreign income or certain investment income

Key Deadlines

Preliminary tax must be at least 90% of your 2027 liability, or 100% of your 2026 liability. If you pay by direct debit, it can instead be 105% of your 2025 liability.

What Expenses Can You Deduct?

You can deduct expenses incurred "wholly and exclusively" for your business, including:

  • Stock, materials and direct costs
  • Business insurance, accountancy and professional fees
  • The business share of motor, phone and home office costs
  • Equipment, through capital allowances (normally 12.5% a year over 8 years)
  • Staff wages and employer PRSI

PRSI and USC for the Self-Employed

Class S PRSI rose from 4.2% to 4.35% on 1 October 2026, and a further increase is scheduled for 1 October 2027.

Budget 2027 for the self-employed

The Earned Income Tax Credit rises to €2,125 and the 20% band to €46,500. Capital Gains Tax falls to 31% from 7 October 2026, and Rent-a-Room relief rises to €16,000. Professional Services Withholding Tax will also move to personalised rates.

Why Use a Tax Agent?
