Income Tax Filing (Form 11)

Expert Form 11 support for the self-employed, landlords and company directors, with your 2026 return and 2027 preliminary tax handled properly.

Updated for Budget 2027

Who needs to file a Form 11?

You must register for income tax and file a Form 11 if you are a chargeable person. This typically includes:

  • Self-employed individuals and sole traders.
  • Landlords receiving rental income.
  • Proprietary directors (owning or controlling more than 15% of a company).
  • PAYE workers with net non-PAYE income of €5,000 or more, or gross non-PAYE income over €30,000.
  • People with foreign income, foreign pensions or dividends.
Budget 2027 update

What changes for the self-employed

  • The Earned Income Tax Credit rises by €125 to €2,125, matching the PAYE credit.
  • The 20% standard rate band rises to €46,500 (single).
  • The 2% USC band is widened to €30,300. The 3% USC surcharge on non-PAYE income over €100,000 continues.
  • Class S PRSI rose to 4.35% on 1 October 2026, with a further rise to 4.5% scheduled for 1 October 2027.
  • Capital Gains Tax falls from 33% to 31% for disposals on or after 7 October 2026 (development land stays at 33%).
  • Professional Services Withholding Tax will move from a flat 20% to personalised rates, subject to a commencement order.
  • The Rent-a-Room tax-free limit rises from €14,000 to €16,000 from 1 January 2027.

If your total income is €13,000 or less, you pay no USC.

Key dates

Your 2026 tax return and 2027 preliminary tax are due by 31 October 2027. If you file and pay online through ROS, Revenue normally extends this to mid-November, and announces the exact date each year.

Late filing triggers automatic surcharges:

  • 5% surcharge (max €12,695) if filed within two months of the deadline.
  • 10% surcharge (max €63,485) if filed more than two months late.
