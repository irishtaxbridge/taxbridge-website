Top Tax Reliefs PAYE Workers Miss Out On in 2027

Updated 12 November 2026 6 min read TaxBridge Team
Updated for Budget 2027

Your standard tax credits are applied automatically, and Budget 2027 increases them to €2,125 each from January. But many valuable reliefs are only granted if you claim them yourself. Here are the ones PAYE workers miss most often, and how much they are worth.

Key Takeaways

  • Medical expenses get 20% tax relief, including physio and non-routine dental
  • Remote workers can claim 30% of electricity, heat and broadband costs
  • Flat-rate expenses apply to many professions automatically, once claimed
  • Pension contributions get relief at your highest rate of tax
  • Rent (€1,150 in 2027) and mortgage interest (final year 2026) are often missed

Relief 1: Medical Expenses

You can claim 20% tax relief on qualifying medical costs for yourself and your dependants, including GP and consultant fees, prescriptions, physiotherapy, and non-routine dental work such as crowns and root canals. Costs refunded by your health insurer do not qualify.

Relief 2: Remote Working Relief

If you work from home, you can claim 30% of your electricity, heating and broadband costs, apportioned to the days you worked at home. Your employer can also pay you up to €3.20 a day tax-free instead.

Relief 3: Flat-Rate Expenses

Many professions, including nurses, teachers, retail staff, healthcare workers and tradespeople, are entitled to a fixed annual deduction for work-related costs such as uniforms and tools. Once claimed through myAccount, it can be backdated for four years.

Relief 4: Tuition Fees

You can claim 20% relief on qualifying third-level tuition fees above a disregard amount. Budget 2027 reduces that disregard to align with the student contribution, so more of your fees will qualify. The student contribution itself also falls by €150 to €2,350.

Relief 5: Pension Contributions (AVCs)

Pension contributions get tax relief at your highest rate of tax, so 40% for many workers. Age-related limits apply, from 15% of earnings under 30 to 40% from age 60, on earnings up to €115,000.
